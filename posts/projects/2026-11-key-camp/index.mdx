**AI fluency curriculum** | November 2026 - Present

## Overview

Key Camp is an AI fluency and creative problem-solving program for grades 3–12. It helps young people use AI agents to turn their own ideas into working projects—not only software, but also art, construction, community history, entrepreneurship, science, and anything else they care about making.

The first pathway begins with the environment where the agent will work: a Raspberry Pi, secure access with Tailscale, OpenClaw, a model, and a communication channel. Once that foundation is ready, the student directs the agent to build a personal digital headquarters: a portfolio for the work that matters to them.

## Scientific method for agentic engineering

Key Camp applies the scientific method to agentic engineering. Students learn to ask a question, make a plan, build with an agent, test the result, share it for feedback, interpret the evidence, and improve the next version.

The goal is not to memorize magic prompts or produce a certain amount of code. Students practice setting goals, giving context, spotting failure, protecting information, evaluating output, and knowing when an agent is wrong.

## Why it exists

The program is grounded in my experience growing up low-income and Native American, with an early focus on serving rural Indigenous communities and inner-city youth. Powerful model subscriptions and technology programs are often inaccessible to the people who could benefit from them most.

Key Camp aims to expand who gets to create with AI—not just who gets trained to code it. The long-term ambition is a teacher-ready AI education publisher and platform with community-specific pathways, but the first release is a focused, static curriculum site.
