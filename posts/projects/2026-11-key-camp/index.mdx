**AI fluency curriculum** | November 2026 - Present

## Overview

Key Camp is a hands-on AI camp for grades 3–12. Students use agents to turn their own ideas into working projects. The project might be art, construction, community history, entrepreneurship, science, or software.

The first pathway begins with the environment where the agent will work: a Raspberry Pi, secure access with Tailscale, OpenClaw, a model, and a communication channel. Once that is ready, the student directs the agent to build a personal digital headquarters: a portfolio for work that matters to them.

## Scientific method for agentic engineering

Key Camp applies the scientific method to agentic engineering. Students learn to ask a question, make a plan, build with an agent, test the result, share it for feedback, interpret the evidence, and improve the next version.

Students do not need to memorize magic prompts or produce a certain amount of code. They practice setting goals, giving context, spotting failure, protecting information, checking output, and knowing when an agent is wrong.

## Why it exists

The program is grounded in my experience growing up low-income and Native American, with an early focus on serving rural Indigenous communities and inner-city youth. Powerful model subscriptions and technology programs are often inaccessible to the people who could benefit from them most.

Key Camp is meant to give more young people a chance to create with AI. The long-term ambition is a teacher-ready education publisher with community-specific pathways. The first release is a focused, static curriculum site.
